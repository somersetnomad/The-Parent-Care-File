It creeps up, then it's all on you

It starts with a login and a form. Then it's the pension credit, the council, a hospital discharge, a care assessment, and a brother who lives "too far away" to help.

Most care organisers you can buy are American. They talk about Medicare and advance directives, and they leave out the UK paperwork that actually decides what help your parent gets.

  • Key detailsNHS number, GP, pharmacy, National Insurance number, who to call first.
  • Medicines and appointmentsA running medicines list and an appointment log you can hand to a paramedic.
  • Power of AttorneyWhich LPAs exist, where the originals are, and a checklist for getting them made.
  • Benefits and deadlinesAttendance Allowance, Carer's Allowance, Pension Credit and Council Tax, with the dates that matter.
  • Council and careCare needs assessment notes, care plan, carers' visit times and contacts.
  • Home and safetyKey safe code location, falls alarm, Priority Services Register, utilities.
  • Hospital staysA grab sheet for admission and a discharge checklist.
  • Sharing it outA one-page rota so siblings can see who does what.

The dates that cost money if you miss them

If your parent is awarded Attendance Allowance and you care for them 35 hours a week or more, the DWP's own notes say you must claim Carer's Allowance within 3 months of the Attendance Allowance decision or you could lose benefit. The file puts that date, and others like it, on the first page.

Source: DWP, AA5 notes (11/25), gov.uk. Rules can change, so the file links to the current gov.uk page for each one.

Who it's for

Women in their 50s and 60s who have become the main carer for a parent, often while working and sometimes while caring for children or grandchildren too.

Useful whether your parent lives with you, down the road, or three hours away.

Questions

Does it cover Scotland and Northern Ireland?

It's written for England and Wales. Power of Attorney works differently in Scotland and Northern Ireland, so those pages point you to the right official service instead.

Is this legal or financial advice?

No. It's an organiser with checklists and links to official guidance from gov.uk, Age UK and Carers UK. For decisions about money or the law, speak to an adviser.

Can I use it on a tablet?

Yes. The PDF is fillable on screen, or you can print it and keep it in a ring binder.
